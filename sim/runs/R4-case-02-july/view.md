## Draft commentary from management

S1. Service revenue grew 5.3 percent on new maintenance contracts in the Northgate and Tri-State territories.

S2. Marketing of $96,400 relates to the regional trade show in September, which is why July is elevated against a quiet June.

S3. Bad debt expense fell 84.7 percent, a reduction of $84,700, as the Coastal Grocers reserve was released.

S4. Warehouse wages rose $74,900 on overtime in the Northgate depot during the summer volume peak.

S5. The decline in cost of product sold is driven by the renegotiated inbound freight rates that took effect on July 1.

S6. Distribution revenue of $5,398,000 was down 3.8 percent from June on the seasonal slowdown in the school nutrition accounts, which is consistent with the same month last year.

S7. Utilities at the depots rose $3,300 because the Southline meter was re-rated onto the summer demand tariff at the start of the month, the Northgate site carried two weeks of chiller load while the dock was closed, and the remaining two depots billed on the usual monthly schedule at unchanged rates.

S8. Freight billed to customers rose $7,500, which fails both legs of the threshold, and the June reclassification of customer freight billings into distribution revenue is complete.

S9. Fleet fuel declined $31,600 as diesel prices came off their June peak.

S10. The depot repair program closed in June and no further repair spend is expected this year.

S11. Repairs of $36,400 in July are the second phase of the dock program at the Southline depot.

S12. The July invoice for interim review fieldwork, roughly $12,800, will arrive in August and be recorded then, which is consistent with our normal practice.
